Practice is the part that gets skipped.

A child who has to work out 7 × 8 has no attention left for the problem it appeared in. A reader still sounding out words is not following the story. Fluency is what frees up thinking.

It comes from short, frequent, accurate repetition — a few minutes most days, at the right level, for long enough to stick. That's unglamorous, hard to keep up alone, and the first thing to fall away in a busy week.

Sensei Classroom is the structure around it: what to practise today, how long for, and what comes next once it's solid.

Three strands, practised together.

Numbers

Arithmetic held in memory rather than worked out each time, then built on steadily until the same confidence carries into algebra.

  • counting
  • number bonds
  • times tables
  • fractions
  • algebra

Words

Sounds before spellings, decoding before speed, and reading before writing — each stage practised until it stops taking effort.

  • phonics
  • decoding

  • writing

Thinking

Short reasoning problems that reward care over speed, so the habit of checking an answer forms alongside the habit of finding one.

  • patterns
  • comparison

  • inference
  • argument

What ten minutes looks like.

  1. Warm up · 2 min

    Yesterday's work, repeated. Short enough to start without negotiation.

  2. Drill · 5 min

    One skill at the edge of what's comfortable, in short reps with immediate feedback.

  3. Stretch · 3 min

    One problem that uses today's skill for something larger, so the practice has a point.

  4. Mark the day

    The session logs itself. You see what's solid, what's slipping, and what comes next.
